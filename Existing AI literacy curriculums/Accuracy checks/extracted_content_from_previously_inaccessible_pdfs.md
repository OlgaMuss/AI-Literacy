## 6. AI Adapt (Gillian Barber) - AI in My Life Taster Teacher Guide

**Source:** `AI Adapt (Gillian Barber) /AI in My Life Taster/AI-in-My-Life-Taster-Teacher-Guide.pdf`

**Suggested Time:** ~1.5 hours

**Curriculum Links:** The interactive and reflective nature ensures students will hone five key skills:
- Information processing
- Critical and creative thinking
- Communicating
- Working with others
- Being personally effective

**Materials Needed:** PowerPoint presentation, Laptop, Screen, Internet Access, Timer. Students will need access to mobile phones or tablets for interactive activities.

**Module Overview:**

1. **Introduction (~3 mins.)**
   - Quick introduction to the module and outline of learning goals

2. **What is AI (~12 mins.)**
   - Short questionnaire on current familiarity with AI
   - Definition of AI
   - Video highlighting benefits and potential drawbacks

3. **Machine Learning (~18 mins.)**
   - What ML is and examples in everyday lives
   - Students try Google's Thing Translator
   - Discussion of facial recognition technology

4. **AI and Bias (~16 mins.)**
   - Potential of AI to exacerbate or eradicate human bias
   - Emotion detection game
   - Reflection on tool accuracy with different faces

5. **Is AI always used for good? (~14 mins.)**
   - AI potential for good at individual and societal level
   - Deepfakes and negative uses
   - Learning to spot Deepfakes
   - Reflection on positive uses

6. **The Importance of Data (~12 mins.)**
   - Value of data
   - Activity on attitudes to data privacy
   - Tips for data control while using AI tools

7. **AI and Ethics (~9 mins.)**
   - Ethical implications of AI development and use
   - Driverless car scenario activity
   - Value assignment in crash scenarios

8. **Recap and Evaluation (~6 mins.)**

**Description:** Comprehensive teacher guide for the taster module with detailed timing and activity descriptions.

## 8. aiEDU - AI Readiness Framework v2.0

**Source:** `aiEDU/aiEDU_AI Readiness Framework_V2.0.pdf`

**Title:** AI Readiness Framework - What students, educators, and leaders need to know v2.0

**Background:**
The AI Education Project (aiEDU) published its first AI Readiness Framework in October 2024. Built on qualitative and quantitative research, the first framework included research-based student, educator AI Readiness competencies, and a district readiness rubric.

**Key Changes in Version 2:**
- Research questioning whether LLM use has negative effects on brain connectivity and cognitive engagement (MIT Media Lab, 2024)
- World Economic Forum's 2025 Jobs Report indicates 22% of all jobs will be disrupted by 2030, with nearly 40% of job skills expected to change
- More students using AI tools for schoolwork (Pew Research, 2025) and companionship (Common Sense Media, 2025)
- Most districts only just starting to develop AI use policies (RAND, 2025)

**New Insights:**
- Human skills—like communication, collaboration, and critical thinking—are increasing in value
- Many technical skills will be augmented with AI
- Requires strong knowledge of foundational skills alongside AI skills
- Educators cannot choose between developing soft skills, core content skills, and AI
- Must take an all-of-the-above approach

**Description:** Comprehensive framework for AI readiness in K-12 education, updated with latest research and labor market data.
