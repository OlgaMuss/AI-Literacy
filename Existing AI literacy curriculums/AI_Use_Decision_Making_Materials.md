# Materials Covering "When to Use and Not to Use AI" in Existing Curriculums

## Executive Summary

This document identifies and analyzes materials from existing AI literacy curriculums that specifically address **decision-making frameworks** for when to use and when not to use AI. The analysis reveals several key approaches and criteria used across different curriculums to help students make informed decisions about AI use.

---

## Key Materials Found

### 1. **aiEDU AI Readiness Framework** - Most Comprehensive Coverage

#### **Student Competencies for Decision-Making:**

**Grades 6-8:**
- **Evaluate when and how AI tools should be used in different situations**
  - Consider if the problem is right for AI
  - Assess if you have enough data
  - Weigh possible risks against benefits

**Grades 9-12:**
- **Critically assess the appropriateness of using AI for novel problems**
  - Analyze ethical implications of AI tool use in various contexts
  - Consider environmental impacts and bias
  - Examine possible use of AI tools at various points in the process

#### **Educator Decision Framework:**
Educators are provided with a **3-criteria framework** for evaluating AI tool use:

1. **Purpose** - Impact on student learning
2. **Privacy** - Data protection considerations  
3. **Practicality** - Feasibility and appropriateness

#### **Specific Decision Criteria:**
- **Compare strengths and weaknesses** of different processes and tool selection for problem solving
- **Ask questions about the problem** to determine what tool (AI or non-AI) would be best and why
- **Identify benefits and drawbacks** of AI tools for a job, considering who or what might be left out or purposefully included

### 2. **aiEDU AP CSP SmartTeach Manual** - Classroom Guidelines

#### **Guidelines for Using AI Code Generators:**

1. **Set Clear Learning Objectives**
   - Clearly define how and why AI code generators are being used
   - Ensure use aligns with learning objectives
   - Communicate that tools supplement learning, not replace understanding

2. **Foster Ethical Use**
   - Emphasize academic honesty
   - Final submissions must be original work reflecting understanding
   - Acknowledge AI assistance transparently

3. **Encourage Critical Engagement**
   - Critically assess AI-generated code
   - Understand why solutions are proposed
   - Identify limitations or errors in AI outputs

#### **Limitations and Considerations:**
- AI should not replace fundamental understanding or manual coding
- Risk of overreliance hindering problem-solving skills development
- Must complement, not replace, traditional learning methods

### 3. **Duke Learning Innovation** - Critical Thinking Focus

#### **"Do We Need AI?" Materials:**
- **Conversation Starters** for discussing AI limitations:
  - Who specifically benefits from AI development? Who is harmed?
  - What are compelling reasons people don't want to use AI?
  - What practical ways could you limit AI use in daily life?
  - Pick a setting where AI use makes you uncomfortable and discuss why

#### **"Does AI Harm Critical Thinking?" Materials:**
- **Key Questions:**
  - What does 'critical thinking' mean? Is it uniquely human?
  - How could overreliance on AI harm critical thinking skills?
  - What strategies balance AI benefits with critical thinking development?
  - How do you evaluate accuracy of AI-generated information?

#### **Active Learning Activities:**
- Compare AI-generated summaries with human-created ones
- Use AI to generate answers, then cross-check with scholarly sources
- Generate arguments for/against controversial topics, then evaluate reasoning
- No AI Alternative: Compare AI vs human-generated arguments without revealing source

### 4. **MIT AI Ethics Curriculum** - Classroom Norms

#### **Lesson 4: Creating Classroom AI Usage Norms**
- **Self-assessment** of AI use patterns
- **Group discussion** on appropriate AI use
- **Drafting AI guidelines** collaboratively
- **Consensus-building** on classroom norms

#### **Key Activities:**
- Present claims in debates about AI strengthening vs weakening connection
- Engage in collaborative consensus-building
- Present proposals clearly for AI use guidelines

## Specific Guidelines Found

### **When TO Use AI:**
- **Supplement learning** without replacing understanding
- **Assist with coding** when stuck on specific problems
- **Generate ideas** for brainstorming and exploration
- **Analyze data** for pattern recognition
- **Provide feedback** on drafts and iterations
- **Automate repetitive tasks** to focus on higher-order thinking

### **When NOT to Use AI:**
- **Replace fundamental understanding** or manual processes
- **Complete assignments** without demonstrating learning
- **Make final decisions** requiring human judgment
- **Handle sensitive personal information** without privacy protections
- **Generate content** without critical evaluation
- **Replace human creativity** and original thinking

### **Red Flags for AI Use:**
- **Overreliance** that hinders skill development
- **Academic dishonesty** or plagiarism concerns
- **Privacy violations** or data security issues
- **Bias reinforcement** or unfair outcomes
- **Environmental costs** outweighing benefits
- **Loss of critical thinking** skills

## Age-Appropriate Progression

### **Elementary (K-5):**
- **Basic tool selection** - Compare different processes for problem solving
- **Simple decision-making** - Identify benefits and drawbacks of different tools
- **Foundation building** - Understand that tools have different purposes

### **Middle School (6-8):**
- **Situational evaluation** - Consider if problem is right for AI
- **Risk-benefit analysis** - Weigh possible risks against benefits
- **Data consideration** - Assess if enough data is available

### **High School (9-12):**
- **Critical assessment** - Analyze appropriateness for novel problems
- **Ethical analysis** - Consider ethical implications in various contexts
- **Environmental awareness** - Factor in environmental impacts
- **Bias evaluation** - Examine potential bias and fairness issues

## Implementation Strategies

### **1. Classroom Norms Development**
- **Collaborative creation** of AI use guidelines
- **Student input** on appropriate vs inappropriate use
- **Consensus-building** on classroom policies
- **Regular review** and updates of guidelines

### **2. Decision-Making Practice**
- **Case studies** with real-world scenarios
- **Role-playing** different decision-making situations
- **Debate activities** on AI use appropriateness
- **Reflection exercises** on personal AI use patterns

### **3. Critical Evaluation Skills**
- **Source verification** of AI outputs
- **Bias detection** in AI-generated content
- **Alternative consideration** - what other tools could be used?
- **Impact assessment** - who benefits, who is harmed?

### **4. Ethical Framework Integration**
- **Values alignment** - does AI use align with personal/school values?
- **Transparency requirements** - when must AI use be disclosed?
- **Accountability measures** - who is responsible for AI-generated content?
- **Fairness considerations** - does AI use create unfair advantages?

## Gaps Identified

### **Missing Elements:**
1. **Quantitative decision frameworks** - Most frameworks are qualitative
2. **Industry-specific guidelines** - Limited sector-specific decision criteria
3. **Cultural considerations** - Most frameworks are Western-focused
4. **Assessment tools** - Limited ways to measure decision-making competency
5. **Parent/guardian involvement** - Few frameworks include family decision-making

### **Areas Needing Development:**
1. **Cost-benefit analysis tools** for AI use decisions
2. **Scenario-based training** for common decision points
3. **Peer evaluation frameworks** for AI use appropriateness
4. **Long-term impact consideration** in decision-making
5. **Cross-cultural perspectives** on AI use appropriateness

---

## Recommendations

### **For Educators:**
1. **Implement the 3-criteria framework** (Purpose, Privacy, Practicality) for all AI tool evaluations
2. **Create classroom norms collaboratively** with students
3. **Practice decision-making** through case studies and scenarios
4. **Regular reflection** on AI use patterns and appropriateness

### **For Students:**
1. **Ask critical questions** before using AI tools
2. **Consider alternatives** to AI for each task
3. **Evaluate outputs critically** rather than accepting blindly
4. **Maintain transparency** about AI use in academic work

### **For Curriculum Developers:**
1. **Develop quantitative frameworks** for AI use decisions
2. **Create industry-specific guidelines** for different career paths
3. **Include cultural perspectives** on AI appropriateness
4. **Design assessment tools** for decision-making competency
5. **Integrate family involvement** in AI use decisions

---

## Conclusion

The existing curriculums provide a solid foundation for teaching students when to use and not to use AI, with the **aiEDU AI Readiness Framework** offering the most comprehensive approach. The key is moving beyond simple "use/don't use" dichotomies to **nuanced decision-making frameworks** that consider multiple factors including learning objectives, ethical implications, privacy concerns, and long-term skill development.

The most effective approaches combine **structured frameworks** with **practical application** through case studies, collaborative norm-setting, and regular reflection on AI use patterns. This prepares students not just for current AI tools, but for the critical thinking skills needed to evaluate future technologies as they emerge.
