## **External Framework Alignment**

### **1. UNESCO AI Competency Framework (2024)**
Maps curriculums against 4 dimensions: **Human-centred mindset, Ethics, Techniques/Applications, and System Design**.
*   **High Alignment**: **MIT (DAILy & Ethics)**, **ENARIS**, **Georgia AI4GA**, and **aiEDU**.
*   **Pedagogical focus**: **Toddle** and **Harvard** focus on the "Mindset" and "Ethics" dimensions for educators.

### **2. UNICEF Policy Guidance (Recommendation 8)**
Focuses on **"Preparing children for present and future developments in AI"**.
*   Most curricula for K-12 (MIT, aiEDU, Common Sense) align strongly by providing foundational literacy and agency.
*   **MIT DAILy** is specifically noted for empowering students as co-creators (YouTube redesign).

### **3. EveryoneAI Risks (Social, Emotional, Cognitive)**
Checks for warnings regarding **emotional dependency, reality dissociation, and social/cognitive risks**.
*   **Covered by**: **MIT**, **Common Sense Media**, **Toddle**, and **AI in My Life**.
*   **Gap**: Technical deep-dives (ENARIS, Georgia) often focus on mechanical bias/ethics rather than psychological/relational risks of AI companions or mimicking systems.

## **Key Findings by Topic**

### **1. Hallucinations**
*   **Covered by**: **Duke**, **MIT (DAILy & AI Ethics)**, and **Berlin**. 
*   **Approach**: **Duke** provides a comprehensive definition ("AI mirage", "bullshit") to avoid anthropomorphizing. **Berlin** frames it as a research question for students. **MIT** includes it in basic vocabulary for educators.
*   **Gap**: Most other curriculums focus on general accuracy but lack specific training on recognizing or understanding LLM hallucinations.

### **2. Biases**
*   **Extensive Coverage**: **ENARIS**, **MIT**, **Duke**, **aiEDU**, and **AI Adapt**.
*   **Themes**: Algorithmic bias (Amazon hiring case study), societal bias, gender and racial bias in facial recognition, and bias in LLM outputs.
*   **Approach**: Often taught through hands-on activities (e.g., training a classifier and seeing it fail) or case studies.

### **3. Ethics**
*   **Universal Core**: Every curriculum listed places a heavy emphasis on ethics.
*   **Specialization**: **MIT** (Ethical Matrices) and **Duke** (Critical Questioning) are primarily ethics-driven.
*   **Integration**: **ENARIS** and **aiEDU** integrate ethics as a horizontal theme across technical modules.

### **4. Technical Aspects**
*   **Highest Depth**: **ENARIS** (detailed modules on Neural Networks, CV, and RL) and **Georgia** (comprehensive course on autonomous systems).
*   **Moderate Depth**: **MIT** (supervised learning mechanics, GANs), **AI Adapt** (ML basics and career pathways), and **aiEDU** (integration with Computer Science Principles).
*   **Conceptual**: **AI4K12** and **Common Sense Media** focus more on the "What" and "Why" than the technical "How".

### **5. When to Use / When Not to Use AI**
*   **Structured Frameworks**: 
    *   **aiEDU**: 3-Criteria Framework (Purpose, Privacy, Practicality) and Student Decision Process (Benefits vs. Risks).
    *   **Duke**: "Do We Need AI?" framework using critical questioning.
    *   **MIT**: Collaborative development of "Classroom AI Usage Norms" and the **YouTube Redesign** activity.
    *   **Toddle**: "Gray Area Sort" activity for ethical decision-making.
