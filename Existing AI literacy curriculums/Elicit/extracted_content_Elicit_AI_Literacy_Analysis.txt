What are existing open source and free AI literacy curriculums for primary,
secondary and high schools? Which countries, language and age group they
cover? what knowledge, competences, skills? How many cover Ethical AI and
risks & benefits of AI?
Ten open source or free AI literacy curricula for K-12 education have been identified, with coverage across all school
levels (primary through high school), originating primarily from the US and Spanish-speaking countries, teaching
machine learning and programming skills through hands-on methods, and universally including AI ethics content
with most addressing bias, societal impacts, and AI risks.

Abstract
Ten open source or free AI literacy curricula for school learners appear in the literature. The studies report curricula
designed for primary, middle, and high school students. Specifically, three curricula target primary students, six
target middle schoolers, and six target high school students—with some spanning multiple levels. Four curricula
originate in the United States, three in Spain (or Spanish‑speaking contexts), one in Australia, one in Europe, and
two provide global or unspecified geographic coverage. Six curricula use English, two use Spanish, and two do not
specify a primary language. One curriculum (Hollands & Breazeal, 2024) is explicitly available under a Creative
Commons license; several others imply open or free status, while five do not mention it.
The curricula address technical and conceptual content that includes machine learning (in five studies) and programming using tools such as Scratch or AppInventor (in three studies). They integrate hands‑on projects, workshops,
and group activities to develop practical competencies alongside conceptual learning. All studies describe coverage
of AI ethics—nine curricula include content on ethical use, bias, and societal impact—while seven address risks such
as misinformation, bias, or legal and sustainability concerns. These findings delineate the current landscape of AI
literacy resources for primary to high school education.

Paper search
Using your research question ”What are existing open source and free AI literacy curriculums for primary, secondary
and high schools? Which countries, language and age group they cover? what knowledge, competences, skills? How
many cover Ethical AI and risks & benefits of AI?”, we searched across over 126 million academic papers from the
Semantic Scholar corpus. We retrieved the 50 papers most relevant to the query.

Screening
We screened in sources that met these criteria:
• Target Population: Does the study describe AI literacy curricula specifically designed for primary, secondary,
or high school students (ages 5-18)?
• Curriculum Accessibility: Are the curricula explicitly described as open source, free, or freely available?
• Curriculum Detail: Does the study provide detailed descriptions of curriculum content, learning objectives,
or educational components?
• Implementation Status: Does the study report on curricula that have been implemented, piloted, or fully
developed (rather than purely theoretical)?

• Educational Level Scope: Is the curriculum NOT designed exclusively for higher education (university/college
level) or adult learners?
• Commercial Status: Is the curriculum NOT a commercial or proprietary AI education program requiring
payment or licensing fees?
• AI Focus Specificity: Is this NOT a general technology or computer science curriculum that only mentions AI
as a minor component?
• Practical Implementation: Is this NOT purely a theoretical framework or conceptual paper without describing
specific curriculum implementations?
We considered all screening questions together and made a holistic judgement about whether to screen in each paper.

Data extraction
We asked a large language model to extract each data column below from each paper. We gave the model the
extraction instructions shown below for each column.
• Target Age Group and Educational Level:
Identify the specific age range or educational level (e.g., primary, middle school, high school) for which the AI
literacy curriculum is designed.
• Look in the introduction, methods, or participant description sections
• If multiple age groups are mentioned, list all
• If an exact age range is not specified, use the educational level as a proxy
• Format: e.g., ”High school students (ages 14-18)” or ”Middle school (grades 6-8)”
• If no clear age group is specified, write ”Not specified”
• Curriculum Coverage of AI Topics:
Identify and list the specific AI knowledge, competencies, and skills covered in the curriculum:
• Scan the methods, curriculum description, and results sections
• List all topics mentioned (e.g., AI basics, machine learning, conversational AI, AI ethics)
• Pay special attention to ethical AI and AI risks/benefits
• Use direct quotes where possible
• If topics are not clearly specified, write ”Not fully described”
• Geographic and Language Coverage:
Determine the country or region where the curriculum was developed or tested:
• Check methods section, participant demographics, and affiliations
• Note the primary language of instruction
• If multiple countries are involved, list all
• If no geographic information is provided, write ”Not specified”
• Format: Country/Region, Primary Language
• Curriculum Delivery Method:
Identify how the AI literacy curriculum was delivered:
• Look for details about instructional approach in methods or implementation sections
• Note specific methods (e.g., online workshop, in-person class, project-based learning)

• Indicate if multiple delivery methods were used
• If delivery method is not clear, write ”Not specified”
• Include any unique pedagogical approaches mentioned
• Accessibility and Inclusivity Features:
Identify any specific efforts to make the curriculum accessible or inclusive:
• Search for mentions of reducing barriers to participation
• Note any strategies to include underrepresented groups
• Look for mentions of minimal prerequisite knowledge or technology requirements
• If no specific accessibility features are mentioned, write ”Not described”
• Use direct quotes if possible to capture the approach
• Sample Size and Participant Characteristics:
Extract information about the study participants:
• Total number of participants
• Breakdown of participants (e.g., students, teachers)
• Any demographic information provided
• If multiple groups were involved (e.g., students and teachers), list separately
• If information is incomplete, note what is missing
• Format: Total n = X (students: Y, teachers: Z)
