# Harvard AI Pedagogy - Learning Points Analysis

## Curriculum Overview
- **Developer:** Harvard Graduate School of Education
- **Format:** Pedagogical methods for AI literacy
- **Target Age:** Secondary/higher education
- **Focus:** Socratic dialogue with AI, prompt engineering, academic integrity, journalism literacy
- **Unique Strengths:** Only depth 3 on playtesting prompts (test to fail), Rogers's active listening for prompting, interview fictional character, 18 journalism pitfalls checklist

## Learning Points Coverage (Depth ≥2)

### Prompt Engineering (Depth 3)
1. **Brockman Prompt** - Breaking complex tasks into smaller steps, full activity with template, playground design, reflection
2. **Computational thinking** - Structuring problems logically for AI (Lesson 4 core)
3. **Playtesting prompts** - Test to pass (easy) vs test to fail (adversarial) full activity
4. **Rogers's active listening rules** - Restate to satisfaction, clarify applied to prompting as protocol

### Academic Integrity (Depth 2-3)
1. **Plagiarism vs AI assistance** (Depth 2) - Assignment integrity, Socratic chats
2. **Communicating AI use** (Depth 3) - Lesson 7 full focus: describe when/how AI influenced process beyond MLA/APA citations
3. **Making thinking visible** (Depth 3) - Worksheets and exit tickets on honest reflection
4. **AI in creative process** (Depth 3) - Lesson 7 full tool and worksheets
5. **Ethical citation** (Depth 2) - Lesson 7 focus

### Critical Thinking & Evaluation (Depth 2-3)
1. **Comparing AI vs human reasoning** (Depth 2) - Blind comparison with rubrics, LLM research on lower cognitive load and narrower analysis
2. **Blind comparison** (Depth 3) - Full rubric creation and comparison cycle activity
3. **Evidence-based reasoning** (Depth 2) - Cross-checking requirement
4. **Critical thinking** (Depth 2) - Socratic method throughout assignment

### Journalism Literacy (Depth 3) - UNIQUE
1. **18 pitfalls checklist** - Full teachable points:
   - Agency attribution (don't say AI "thinks" or "wants")
   - Humanoid imagery (avoid misleading anthropomorphic images)
   - Brain analogies (neural networks ≠ human brains)
   - Human comparisons (avoid "like a human")
   - Hyperbole ("revolutionary" claims)
   - False claims (fact-check capabilities)
   - Conflict of interest (disclose affiliations)
   - Missing limitations (always include what AI can't do)
   - Hidden labor (ghost work, data labeling)
   - Buried limitations (don't hide caveats)
   - Plus 8 more pitfalls

### Socratic Dialogue (Depth 2-3)
1. **Socratic chats** (Depth 2) - Emphasize authorial agency, preserve student voice
2. **Interview fictional character** (Depth 3) - Full activity: open questions, journalistic principles, evidence from transcript vs source book with rubric
3. **AI-free writing cycles** (Depth 3) - Pen-and-paper first, then with AI, compare processes

### Using AI for Learning (Depth 2-3)
1. **When AI helps vs hinders** (Depth 2) - Writing cycles comparison
2. **Authorial agency** (Depth 2) - Socratic chats emphasize
3. **Metacognition** (Depth 2) - Throughout lessons
4. **LLMs vs search engines** (Depth 2) - Lesson 4 distinction with tradeoffs

### Creativity (Depth 2)
1. **Supporting creativity while maintaining voice** - Essay revision emphasis
2. **Personas for writing** (Depth 2) - Informative, persuasive, entertainment worksheets
3. **AI as creative tool** (Depth 2) - Creative process worksheets

### Other Topics (Depth 2)
1. **Decomposing problems** - Lesson 4 focus
2. **Human advantages** (Depth 2) - Creativity, empathy harder to automate
3. **Overreliance critique** (Depth 2) - Research on reasoning quality

- Socratic dialogue emphasis
- Evidence-based reasoning
- Rubric creation for evaluation

- Active listening protocols
- Adversarial thinking (test to fail)
