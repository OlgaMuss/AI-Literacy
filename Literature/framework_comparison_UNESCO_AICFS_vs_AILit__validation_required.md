## 1. Provenance and purpose

| | UNESCO AI CFS (2024) | AILit (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Issuer | UNESCO | Joint European Commission + OECD; supported by CodeAI; approved by PISA Governing Board 2026-04-08 |
| Function | Curricular reference for national agencies | Common language for AI literacy + direct input to **PISA 2029 MAIL** assessment (p. 2) |
| Development | Expert consultation | Literature review + expert group + **international public consultation on the May 2025 draft: 2,000+ respondents, 100+ countries, 41% teachers** (Annex 1) |
| Binding force | Non-binding | Non-binding; "Adaptation of the framework content is both expected and encouraged" (p. 6) |

## 2. Architecture

| | UNESCO AI CFS | AILit (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Top level | 4 **aspects**: Human-centred mindset; Ethics of AI; AI techniques and applications; AI system design | 4 **domains**: Engage with AI; Create with AI; Manage AI; Shape AI |
| Units | 12 **competency blocks** (aspect × level matrix) | **19 competences** (Engage 7, Create 4, Manage 4, Shape 4) |
| Progression | 3 levels: Understand → Apply → Create. 'Apply' = expected exit for most students; 'Create' elective (Ch. 3.2, pp. 20–22) | 3 **Learner Expectations** per competence: Basic → Intermediate → Advanced, each with an "In the Classroom" scenario |
| Knowledge base | Embedded in competency blocks | Separate **Knowledge** section: 4 topics (Nature of AI 1.1–1.5; Human choices 2.1–2.5; Capabilities & limitations 3.1–3.4; Role in society 4.1–4.3), each statement referenced |
| Transversal | — | 7 **Skills** (Collaboration, Creativity, Critical Thinking, Computational Thinking, Self & Social Awareness, Problem Solving, Communication) + 6 **Attitudes** (Reflective, Responsible, Curious, Innovative, Adaptable, Empathetic) |
| Ethics | **Standalone aspect** (Embodied ethics → Safe and responsible use → Ethics by design) | Deliberately **woven through all domains**, not a category: "ethical considerations cannot be separated from an understanding of the systems themselves" (p. 15) |
| Building AI | "AI system design" aspect: problem scoping, architecture design, iteration — learners create AI artefacts | Shape AI is evaluative/propositional (investigate, evaluate, design-with-data-attention, improve); "The goal of this domain is not for learners to develop commercial products" (p. 40) |

## 3. The age-specificity question (core to our paper)

Both frameworks **refuse to map content to ages or grades** — but in different ways:

- **UNESCO CFS:** progression is by *mastery level* (Understand/Apply/Create), and age mapping is explicitly delegated: "It is up to national or institutional curriculum agencies to define concrete learning objectives for specific student cohorts, based on their AI readiness" (Ch. 4, p. 27).
- **AILit 2026:** progression levels were **added** in response to stakeholder demand (Annex 1 theme "Levels of Progression and Realistic Implementation"), but: "These progression levels apply to primary and secondary education outcomes, but **do not correspond to specific age ranges or grade levels**" (p. 25). Teachers are "encouraged to use these Learner Expectations to build their own evaluation rubrics" (p. 25).

**Consequence for our claim:** "frameworks are not age-specific" remains **true** for both, but the published AILit is more operational than the 2025 draft we initially critiqued: proficiency progressions + classroom scenarios now exist. The residual gap is precise: no age/grade banding, no timing/duration guidance, no learning-point-level content specification, and rubric construction is offloaded to teachers. Our learning-points approach answers exactly this gap.

## 5. Draft (2025) → published (2026): what changed and why

### 5.1 Structural changes

| Change | Draft (May 2025) | Published (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Competence count | **22** ("The four domains encompass 22 competences", draft p. 25) | **19** (Engage 7, Create 4, Manage 4, Shape 4) |
| 4th domain | **Designing AI** | **Shape AI** (same hands-on intent, broader framing) |
| Domain naming | Engaging / Creating / Managing / Designing (gerunds) | Engage / Create / Manage / Shape (imperatives) |
| Scenarios | **Primary vs Secondary** education scenarios per competence | **Basic / Intermediate / Advanced** Learner Expectations + "In the Classroom" scenarios; explicitly *not* age-banded |
| Knowledge topics | 5 topics (K1–K5; K5 = ethics) | 4 topics; ethics dissolved into all (per p. 15 policy); environmental statement 1.5 added |

### 5.3 Does the OECD doc explain why?

**Partially.** It documents *themes*, not a line-item changelog:
- **Annex 1** (Summary of Feedback) lists five revision drivers: (1) **metacognition** & relational AI; (2) **student agency** + more hands-on sense-making; (3) **"discerning capabilities"** — reviewers felt the draft "painted an overly positive picture of AI"; (4) **environment**; (5) **levels of progression** for implementation/localisation.
- **Annex 2** credits the ETS K–12 report (Chakraburty et al., 2025) for the Learner Expectations progressions, Digital Promise for competence framing, DigComp for the K/S/A structure, UNESCO CFS/CFT for "global relevance and implementation".
- **Not explained:** the specific 22→19 consolidation, the individual merges/moves/drops, and the Designing→Shape rename are not justified competence-by-competence. The consultation report describes *what themes drove revision*, not *which editorial decisions* followed. (This opacity is itself relevant to our paper: even a 2,000-respondent international framework revision leaves no machine-readable trail of design decisions — curricula-as-data argument.)

## 6. Implications for our paper

1. **Intro claim update (precision):** replace "frameworks are competence-based and not age-specific" with: both flagship frameworks explicitly delegate age/grade mapping and rubric construction to teachers/local agencies (UNESCO Ch. 4 p. 27; AILit p. 25), and AILit states adaptation is "both expected and encouraged" (p. 6). The gap is not progression (AILit now has it) but **operationalization at learning-point level for a specific age band**.
2. **Our concept mapping:** `curriculum_concepts__validation_required.csv` currently maps only to UNESCO CFS blocks and PISA MAIL — **no AILit columns were ever added** (the "22 competences" figure lived only in the 2025 draft and in our discussion notes). If we add an AILit mapping, it must use the **published 19-competence numbering** above.
3. **AILit's own history supports our thesis:** frameworks iterate under stakeholder pressure (22→19 in one year), each iteration is a manual, expert-heavy process, and the revision rationale is documented only narratively — the loop we propose to make data-driven and LLM-accelerated.
