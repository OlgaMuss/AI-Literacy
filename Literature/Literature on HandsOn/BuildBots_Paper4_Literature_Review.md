## 1. Categorization Framework: Types of Hands-On Activities

### Five Distinct Activity Types

| Type | Physical Engagement | Ownership | Technical Understanding | Anthropomorphism | Time | Difficulty | BuildBots |
|------|-------------------|-----------|------------------------|------------------|------|-----------|-----------|
| **Creating** | Very High | Very High | Very High | Low | Very High | Very High | - |
| **Assembling** | High | High | High | Low-Medium | High | Medium | Experimental (W1-3) |
| **Tinkering** | High | High | Very High | Low | Medium-High | Medium | - |
| **Programming** | Low-Medium | Medium | High | Medium | Medium-High | Medium-High | Both (W4-6) |
| **Interacting** | Very Low | Low | Low | High | Low | Low | Control (W1-3) |

#### 1. Creating/Building from Scratch
- Designing without predefined plans
- Maximum creative decision-making
- Highest ownership and self-efficacy
- Deepest understanding of mechanical principles

#### 2. Assembling from Instructions ⭐ *BuildBots Experimental*
- Following step-by-step instructions from kit
- Moderate creative freedom (minor modifications)
- **IKEA effect**: Effort → value, sense of accomplishment
- **Demystification**: Understanding structure, reduced "black box"
- Example: Building Marty robot from manual

#### 3. Tinkering/Modifying
- Taking apart, reassembling, customizing
- Exploratory, playful, "failing forward"
- Hypothesis testing through iteration
- Deep understanding through experimentation

#### 4. Programming
- Writing code for robot behavior
- Algorithmic thinking, debugging
- Can be abstract (simulator) or concrete (physical robot)
- Understanding of robot "intelligence"

#### 5. Interacting/Using ⭐ *BuildBots Control*
- Using pre-assembled, pre-programmed robot
- Focus on social/functional interaction
- Minimal technical understanding
- "Black box" perception persists
- May increase anthropomorphism

### Key Insight for BuildBots
**Your study tests "Assembly vs. Interaction" NOT "Building vs. Interaction"**
- Experimental group: Follows instructions (assembly)
- NOT creating from scratch (building)
- Important terminology clarification for paper

### Additional Dimensions for Categorizing Activities

**Robot Autonomy Level** (What the robot does, not what learner does):
1. **Teleoperated**: Direct human control
2. **Semi-Autonomous**: Follows programmed sequences
3. **Autonomous**: Makes independent decisions
4. **Adaptive**: Learns and changes behavior
- *Note*: Affects learning about AI concepts; BuildBots uses semi-autonomous (programmed sequences)

**Activity Focus** (Pedagogical intent):
- **Expression-focused**: Arts & Bots, humanities integration
- **Task-focused**: Competitive robotics, problem-solving challenges
- **Exploration-focused**: Open-ended discovery, tinkering
- **Skill-focused**: Learning specific technical skills

## 2. Core Literature

### Foundational Theory

**Papert, S. (1980). Children, Computers, and Powerful Ideas. Eugene, OR, USA.**
- Foundational work on constructionism
- Learning through building and creating
- Emphasis on "objects to think with"

**Piaget, J. (1975). L'équilibration des structures cognitives. PUF, Paris.**
- Constructivist theory foundation
- Knowledge construction through active engagement

**Ackermann, E. K. (2004). Constructing knowledge and transforming the world. A learning zone of one's own: Sharing representations and flow in collaborative learning environments, 1, 15-37.**
- Reviews constructionism vs constructivism
- Emphasis on learning through making

### Meta-Analyses: Hands-On Learning Effectiveness

**Freeman, S. L., Eddy, S. L., McDonough, M., Smith, M. K., Okoroafor, N., Jordt, H., & Wenderoth, M. P. (2014). Active learning increases student performance in science, engineering, and mathematics. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 111(23), 8410-8415.** ⭐
- META-ANALYSIS: Hands-on learning improves student performance compared to traditional lecturing
- Science, engineering, and mathematics domains
- *Supports H1-2 (engagement, subjective learning)*

**Ouyang & Xu (2024). Didactic impact of educational robotics on the development of STEM competence in primary education: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Frontiers in Education, 9, 1480908.** ⭐ [ACQUIRE]
- Meta-analysis confirms positive effects of educational robotics
- Comparison: robotics vs on-screen programming
- Robotics provides higher motivation, practical context, interdisciplinary engagement
- Open access: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2024.1480908/full
- *Supports H1-2, H4-6 (learning outcomes)*

**Bai & Tian (2025). Educational robotics may enhance students' conceptual knowledge, applied skills, and learning attitude in STEM education: A meta-analysis. Frontiers in Education.** [Check publication status]
- Moderate to significant positive effects on STEM learning
- Enhances: conceptual knowledge, applied skills, learning attitudes
- Caution: self-developed tools may inflate effect sizes
- *Supports H4-6 (objective learning)*

### AI/Robotics Specific

**Hitron et al. (2019). CHI, 1-11.** ⭐⭐ KEY PAPER
- Hands-on ML experience → better understanding of concepts
- **Demystification**: Reduces "black box" thinking
- Enhanced understanding of robots as machines vs social entities
- *Supports H4-6 (learning), H7 (competence), H11 (anthropomorphism)*

**Williams et al. (2019). AAAI, 33(01), 9729-9736.** ⭐⭐ KEY PAPER
- Pre-schoolers (4-6 years): Building + programming → AI concept understanding
- **Knowledge affects perception**: Higher knowledge → robots as people not toys
- Higher knowledge → less likely think robots smarter than them
- *Supports H4-6 (learning), H7, H11 (prior knowledge moderates perception)*

**Williams et al. (2024). HRI, 772-780.**
- Doodlebot: AI literacy through robot building

**Druga et al. (2019). FabLearn, 104-111.**
- Inclusive AI literacy for children

**Diamond, A., Lee, C., Senften, P., Lam, A., & Abbott, D. (2019). Randomized control trial of Tools of the Mind: Marked benefits to kindergarten children and their teachers. PLOS ONE, 14(9), e0222447.**
- Benefits of hands-on learning for young children
- Sitting and listening for extended periods is difficult for children
- *Supports H1 (engagement with children)*

### Ownership Effects: IKEA Effect

**Norton et al. (2012). J Consumer Psychology, 22(3), 453-460.** ⭐⭐ FOUNDATIONAL
- **IKEA effect**: Labor → love, higher satisfaction with self-assembled items
- Mediated by ownership + accomplishment
- *Supports H12-13 (attachment, intention)*

**Franke et al. (2010). Management Science, 56(1), 125-140.**
- "I Designed It Myself" effect
- Higher perceived value when feeling like creator
- *Supports H12-13*

**Sun & Sundar (2016). HRI, 189-196.** ⭐⭐ KEY PAPER
- Self-assembly → more positive robot evaluation
- Mediated by **ownership + accomplishment**
- *Supports H8 (warmth), H12-13*

**Huang et al. (2013). HFES, 57(1), 1825-1829.** ⭐
- LEGO robot building → self-efficacy, interest, attachment
- Problem-solving with direct feedback
- Creative process (not duty) → more effort
- Negative feelings when dismantling (time/effort invested)
- *Supports H1-2 (engagement), H12-13*

**Groom et al. (2009). HRI, 31-36.** ⭐
- Self-built vs other-assembled comparison
- Lower maliciousness, higher friendliness, attachment, team feelings
- *Supports H8 (warmth), H12-13*

**IKEA effect & robots (2024). PMC 12240306.** ⭐⭐ RECENT [ACQUIRE]
- Assembly as empathy formation mechanism
- Building → positive relationship with technology
- Long-term engagement and AI acceptance
- Open access: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12240306/
- *Supports H8, H12-13*

### Cognitive Development

**Robotics & Cognitive Development (2023). PMC 9988604.** [ACQUIRE]
- Eye-tracking + cognitive assessments with children
- Improves visuospatial working memory, logical reasoning
- Robotics as "game play" → increased engagement and focus
- Open access: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9988604/
- *Supports H1-2 (engagement), H4-6 (learning)*

### Taxonomies

**Zhong, B., Liu, X., Xia, L., & Sun, W. (2022). A Proposed Taxonomy of Teaching Models in STEM Education: Robotics as an Example. SAGE Open, 12(2).** ⭐⭐ CRITICAL [ACQUIRE]
- **Two-dimensional taxonomy**: Learning outcomes (product vs knowledge) × Teaching process (forward vs reverse)
- **Four teaching models identified:**
  1. **Project-Based Learning (PBL)**: Inventing with robots (product-oriented, forward)
  2. **Reverse Engineering (RE)**: Imitating and redesigning (product-oriented, reverse)
  3. **Scientific Inquiry (SI)**: Problem exploration and inquiry (knowledge-oriented, forward)
  4. **Troubleshooting/Debugging (T/D)**: Solving predetermined problems (knowledge-oriented, reverse)
- Open access: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21582440221099525

**Wang, X., et al. (2024). Physical Robots in Education: A Systematic Review Based on the Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge Framework. Sustainability, 16(12), 4987.** [ACQUIRE]
- TPCK-based framework for educational robotics
- Teaching strategies categorized: physical interaction (displaying gestures, learner control), communication, role play, collaborative learning
- Open access: https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/16/12/4987

**Engineering Design Process Categories** (from Co-robotics and educational resources):
Five distinct stages of robotics activities:
1. **Design**: Brainstorming, sketching, conceptualizing the robot
2. **Production**: Creating components (3D modeling/printing)
3. **Assembly**: Putting together mechanical structures and electronic components
4. **Programming**: Writing code to control robot behavior
5. **Test and Validation**: Troubleshooting, debugging, iterating on design

**Core Technical Domains** (from educational resources):
Three fundamental areas of robotics learning:
1. **Building & Mechanics**: Construction, structural integrity, gears, motors, wheels for movement
2. **Electronics & Sensors**: Circuits, buttons, lights, sensors (proximity, light, infrared) for environment interaction
3. **Programming & Control**: Algorithmic thinking, logic, sequencing to control robot behavior

**Building vs. Assembly Distinctions** (from recent robotics literature 2022-2026):
- **Building**: The broader, generative process of creating a system
  - System creation, structural integrity, generative design
  - Fabrication-aware design and structural optimization
  - Robot may act as builder of other robots/complex systems (e.g., MIT assembler robots)
- **Assembling**: Task-oriented manipulation and aggregation of components
  - Component aggregation, sequencing, precision placement
  - Assembly grammar, peg-in-hole strategies, relative positioning
  - Robot acts as manipulator of parts (voxels, screws, plates)
- **Note**: Technical robotics literature distinguishes these, but educational literature often uses terms interchangeably

### Embodied & Tangible Learning

**Tangible User Interfaces (TUIs) & Embodied Learning Literature:**
- **Embodied Learning**: Physical engagement, sensory interaction, kinesthetic learning
- Leverages posture, gesture, eye contact, physical manipulation to bridge theoretical knowledge with perception
- **TUIs**: Embed technology into physical objects for natural actions (grabbing, moving)
- Communicates abstract concepts through physical manipulatives and embodied metaphors
- **Benefits**: Scaffolds learning, improves meta-cognition, fosters positive learning emotions
- Reduces cognitive load by using external physical resources to simplify problem-solving
- **Theoretical grounding**: Piaget, Vygotsky constructivist theories
- Robotics particularly effective: requires hands-on skills (building, programming, operating hardware)
- Improves motor skills, memory retention, student engagement vs traditional lectures

### Maker Education

**Martinez, S. L., & Stager, G. Invent to Learn: Making, Tinkering, and Engineering in the Classroom.** [Book - foundational text]
- **Making**: Goal-oriented process of designing and building specific projects to demonstrate/deepen understanding
- **Tinkering**: Process-oriented philosophy celebrating discovery through playful exploration, rapid prototyping
  - Emphasizes process over product, inquiry-driven
  - "Failing forward" and using materials in unconventional ways

**Wilkinson, K., & Petrich, M. The Art of Tinkering.** [Book]
- Defines tinkering approach in hands-on learning

**Maker Education Core Elements:**
- **Maker Mindset**: Disposition valuing playfulness, experimentation, iteration, collaboration, community
- **Makerspaces**: Physical/virtual environments with tools (3D printers, Arduino, microcontrollers)
- **Facilitation**: Educator shifts from content deliverer to facilitator supporting student agency
- **Assessment**: Embedded, performance-based, focusing on process, collaboration, soft skills (not just final artifact)
- **Pedagogical Approaches**: Project-Based Learning, Inquiry-Based Learning, Design Thinking, Constructionism

**Maker Ed Resources** [Open access: makered.org]
- Frameworks for activity design and facilitation
- Youth Makerspace Playbook and practical guides

## 4. Key Mechanisms by Activity Type

### Assembly (BuildBots Experimental)
**Psychological Mechanisms:**
- IKEA effect: Effort → perceived value
- Sense of accomplishment: Completed functional artifact
- Demystification: Seeing internal structure
- Moderate ownership: "I built this"

**Predicted Outcomes:**
- ✓ Higher technical understanding (exposed to structure)
- ✓ Higher ownership & attachment (effort invested)
- ✓ Lower anthropomorphism (see as machine)
- ✓ More realistic perception (understand limitations)
- ✓ Higher self-efficacy (accomplished task)

### Interaction (BuildBots Control)
**Psychological Mechanisms:**
- Social engagement: Focus on communicative features
- Immediate gratification: Low barrier to entry
- Black box thinking: Internal workings hidden

**Predicted Outcomes:**
- ✓ Lower technical understanding (no exposure to structure)
- ✓ Lower ownership (not involved in creation)
- ✓ Higher anthropomorphism (focus on social features)
- ✓ Less realistic perception (may overestimate)
- ✓ Higher immediate engagement (social interaction rewarding)

## 5. Research Gaps & BuildBots Contributions

### Gaps in Literature

1. **Limited children-focused research**  
   Most IKEA effect studies use adults; few examine children's attachment

2. **Building vs assembly undefined**  
   Terms used interchangeably; need clear distinctions

3. **Long-term effects unknown**  
   Short-term studies; need lasting effects on literacy, attachment, engagement

4. **Knowledge × perception interaction**  
   Williams (2019) shows prior knowledge affects perception; need more on how building moderates this

5. **No clear activity taxonomy**  
   Need framework for learner's physical engagement levels

### BuildBots Contributions

✓ **Children-focused** (vs adults)  
✓ **Clear distinction**: Assembly vs Interaction  
✓ **Longitudinal**: Pre/mid/post + retention tests  
✓ **Multiple outcomes**: Learning, perception, attachment  
✓ **Moderating effects**: Prior knowledge exploration  
✓ **Real classroom context**: Ecological validity  
✓ **Clarifies terminology**: Assembly (not building from scratch)

## 7. Practical Implications: Choosing Activity Type

**Use Creating/Building when:**
- Goal: Innovation, creativity, design thinking
- Students have prior assembly experience
- Time and resources available for extended projects
- Failure is acceptable/encouraged

**Use Assembling when:** ⭐ *BuildBots Experimental*
- Goal: Understanding structure and function
- Introduction to robotics
- Limited time or resources
- Need structured learning experience
- Want to ensure successful completion

**Use Tinkering when:**
- Goal: Deep exploration and discovery
- Encouraging experimentation
- "Safe to fail" environment available
- Developing troubleshooting skills

**Use Programming when:**
- Goal: Computational thinking and coding skills
- Physical robot may or may not be available
- Focus on behavior rather than structure

**Use Interaction when:** ⭐ *BuildBots Control*
- Goal: Social learning, communication skills
- Introduction to robots (build interest)
- Physical limitations prevent building
- Focus on human-robot interaction concepts

**Recommended Sequence for Curricula:**
1. Interaction (brief) → Generate interest
2. Assembly → Understand structure
3. Programming → Add behavior
4. Tinkering → Modify and explore
5. Creating → Apply knowledge to novel design

*BuildBots sequence:* Assembly OR Interaction (3 weeks) → Programming (3 weeks)

## 8. Contribution Statement for Paper

*"While prior work demonstrates benefits of hands-on robotics activities, few studies systematically compare different levels of physical engagement—particularly distinguishing **assembly** (following instructions) from **building** (creative design) and **interaction** (using pre-built robots). Existing research on ownership effects (IKEA effect) primarily focuses on adults, leaving children's experiences underexplored. Furthermore, the interaction between hands-on engagement and children's developing understanding of AI/robot capabilities remains unclear. The BuildBots study addresses these gaps by comparing robot assembly with robot interaction in a longitudinal, classroom-based study with children, examining effects on learning outcomes, robot perception, and attachment."*
