## Papers from Pre-registration

### Foundational Theory: Constructionism & Constructivism

**1. Papert, S. (1980). Children, Computers, and Powerful Ideas. Eugene, OR, USA.**
- Foundational work on constructionism
- Learning through building and creating
- Emphasis on "objects to think with"

**2. Piaget, J. (1975). L'équilibration des structures cognitives. PUF, Paris.**
- Constructivist theory foundation
- Knowledge construction through active engagement

**3. Ackermann, E. K. (2004). Constructing knowledge and transforming the world. A learning zone of one's own: Sharing representations and flow in collaborative learning environments, 1, 15-37.**
- Reviews constructionism vs constructivism
- Emphasis on learning through making

### Hands-On Learning Effects: General

**4. Freeman, S. L., Eddy, S. L., McDonough, M., Smith, M. K., Okoroafor, N., Jordt, H., & Wenderoth, M. P. (2014). Active learning increases student performance in science, engineering, and mathematics. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 111(23), 8410-8415.**
- META-ANALYSIS: Hands-on learning improves student performance
- Compared to traditional lecturing
- Science, engineering, and mathematics domains

**5. Diamond, A., Lee, C., Senften, P., Lam, A., & Abbott, D. (2019). Randomized control trial of Tools of the Mind: Marked benefits to kindergarten children and their teachers. PLOS ONE, 14(9), e0222447.**
- Benefits of hands-on learning for young children
- Sitting and listening for extended periods is difficult for children

### Hands-On Learning: AI & Robotics Specific

**6. Hitron, T., Orlev, Y., Wald, I., Shamir, A., Erel, H., & Zuckerman, O. (2019). Can Children Understand Machine Learning Concepts?: The Effect of Uncovering Black Boxes. In Proceedings of the 2019 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems, 1-11.**
- **KEY PAPER**: Learning through experience supports understanding of ML concepts
- Better understanding of AI through hands-on activities
- Reduces "black box" thinking
- Enhanced understanding of robots as machines vs. social entities

**7. Williams, R., Ali, S., Alcantara, R., Burghleh, T., Alghowinem, S., & Breazeal, C. (2024). Doodlebot: An Educational Robot for Creativity and AI Literacy. In Proceedings of the 2024 ACM/IEEE International Conference on Human-Robot Interaction, 772-780.**
- Recent work on AI literacy through robot building

**8. Williams, R., Park, H. W., Oh, L., & Breazeal, C. (2019). PopBots: Designing an Artificial Intelligence Curriculum for Early Childhood Education. Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence, 33(01), 9729-9736.**
- **KEY PAPER**: Building and programming activity supported AI concept understanding
- Pre-schoolers (4-6 years)
- Children's AI knowledge affected perception: higher knowledge → see robots as people rather than toys
- Higher knowledge → less likely to think robots are smarter than them

**9. Druga, S., Vu, S. T., Likhith, E., & Qiu, T. (2019). Inclusive AI literacy for kids around the world. In Proceedings of FabLearn 2019, 104-111.**
- Hands-on AI literacy for children

### Robot Building: Attachment & Ownership Effects

**10. Huang, L., Varnado, T., & Gillan, D. (2013). An Exploration of Robot Builders' Attachment to Their LEGO Robots. Proceedings of the Human Factors and Ergonomics Society Annual Meeting, 57(1), 1825-1829.**
- **KEY PAPER**: Building creates problem-solving situations with direct feedback
- Associated with increased self-efficacy and interest
- Described as creative process rather than duty → more effort
- Feelings of attachment and negative feelings when dismantling
- Due to time and effort invested

**11. Groom, V., Takayama, L., Ochi, P., & Nass, C. (2009). I am my robot: the impact of robot-building and robot form on operators. In Proceedings of the 4th ACM/IEEE international conference on Human robot interaction, 31-36.**
- **KEY PAPER**: Self-built robot vs. other-assembled robot comparison
- Lower maliciousness ratings
- Higher friendliness ratings
- Increased self-extension, attachment, and team feelings

### Ownership Effects: IKEA Effect

**12. Norton, M. I., Mochon, D., & Ariely, D. (2012). The IKEA effect: When labor leads to love. Journal of Consumer Psychology, 22(3), 453-460.**
- **FOUNDATIONAL**: Higher satisfaction with self-assembled furniture
- Mediated by sense of ownership and feelings of accomplishment

**13. Franke, N., Schreier, M., & Kaiser, U. (2010). The "I Designed It Myself" Effect in Mass Customization. Management Science, 56(1), 125-140.**
- Higher perceived value when feeling like creator
- Mediated by sense of ownership and feelings of accomplishment

**14. Sun, Y., & Sundar, S. S. (2016). Psychological importance of human agency: how self-assembly affects user experience of robots. In 2016 11th ACM/IEEE International Conference on Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), 189-196.**
- **KEY PAPER**: Self-assembly affects robot evaluation
- Both robot and interaction evaluated more positively
- Mediated by sense of ownership and sense of accomplishment

## Additional Key Papers - Categorization of Hands-On Activities

### Taxonomies of Hands-On Activities in Robotics

**15. Zhong, B., Liu, X., Xia, L., & Sun, W. (2022). A Proposed Taxonomy of Teaching Models in STEM Education: Robotics as an Example. SAGE Open, 12(2).**
- **CRITICAL TAXONOMY PAPER**: Two dimensions framework
  - **Learning outcomes**: Product-oriented vs. Knowledge-oriented
  - **Teaching process**: Forward vs. Reverse teaching
- Four teaching models identified:
  1. **Project-Based Learning (PBL)**: Inventing with robots
  2. **Reverse Engineering (RE)**: Imitating and redesigning existing products
  3. **Scientific Inquiry (SI)**: Problem exploration and inquiry procedures
  4. **Troubleshooting/Debugging (T/D)**: Solving predetermined problems through error correction

**16. Engineering Design Process Categories**
From literature (Co-robotics paper and others):
Five distinct categories of robotics activities:
1. **Design**: Brainstorming, sketching, conceptualizing
2. **Production**: Creating components (3D modeling/printing)
3. **Assembly**: Putting together mechanical structures and electronic components
4. **Programming**: Writing code to control robot behavior
5. **Test and Validation**: Troubleshooting, debugging, iterating

### Core Technical Domains

**17. Three Fundamental Technical Domains** (from educational resources):
1. **Building and Mechanics**: Construction, structural integrity, gears, motors, wheels
2. **Electronics and Sensors**: Circuits, buttons, lights, sensors (proximity, light, infrared)
3. **Programming and Control**: Algorithmic thinking, logic, sequencing

### Building vs. Assembly Distinctions

**18. Recent Literature on Building vs. Assembly** (2022-2026):
- **Building**: The broader, generative process of creating a system
  - System creation, structural integrity, generative design
  - Fabrication-aware design and structural optimization
  - Robot may act as builder of other robots/complex systems
  
- **Assembling**: Task-oriented manipulation and aggregation of components
  - Component aggregation, sequencing, precision placement
  - Assembly grammar, peg-in-hole strategies, relative positioning
  - Robot acts as manipulator of parts (voxels, screws, plates)

### Manipulation Taxonomies

**19. Bullock, I. M., et al. (2013). A Hand-Centric Classification of Human and Robot Dexterous Manipulation. IEEE Transactions on Haptics.**
- Hand-centric manipulation taxonomy
- Classifies based on: contact, prehensile vs. non-prehensile, specific motions
- Independent of specific hand hardware

**20. Wang, X., et al. (2024). Physical Robots in Education: A Systematic Review Based on the Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge Framework. Sustainability, 16(12), 4987.**
- TPCK-based framework for educational robotics
- Categories: physical interaction, communication, role play, collaborative learning

### Meta-Analyses: Educational Robotics Effectiveness

**21. Bai & Tian (2025). Educational robotics may enhance students' conceptual knowledge, applied skills, and learning attitude in STEM education: A meta-analysis.**
- Frontiers in Education (anticipated publication)
- Moderate to significant positive effects on STEM learning
- Enhances: conceptual knowledge, applied skills, learning attitudes

**22. Ouyang & Xu (2024). Didactic impact of educational robotics on the development of STEM competence in primary education: a systematic review and meta-analysis.**
- Frontiers in Education, 9, 1480908
- Confirms positive effects of educational robotics
- Comparison: robotics vs. on-screen programming
- Robotics provides higher motivation, practical context, interdisciplinary engagement

### Cognitive Development

**23. Effects of Robotics Education on Young Children's Cognitive Development (2023). PMC 9988604.**
- Eye-tracking and cognitive assessments (Corsi and Raven tests)
- Improves visuospatial working memory
- Enhances logical/abstract reasoning skills
- Children perceive robotics as "game play" → increased engagement

### IKEA Effect in Educational Context

**24. IKEA effect and empathy for robots: Can assembly strengthen human-agent relationships? (2024). PMC 12240306.**
- **RECENT PAPER**: Assembly as bottom-up mechanism for empathy formation
- Building robots increases positive relationship with technology
- Improves long-term engagement and acceptance of AI systems
- Student agency: planning how to achieve learning goals

### Embodied and Tangible Learning

**25. Effects of physically embodied educational robots (2026). Journal of Educational Psychology (anticipated).**
- Physical engagement, sensory interaction, kinesthetic learning
- Leverages posture, gesture, eye contact, physical manipulation
- Bridges theoretical knowledge with perception

**26. Tangible User Interfaces (TUIs) Literature**
- Embeds technology into physical objects
- Natural actions: grabbing, moving items
- Communicates abstract concepts through physical manipulatives
- Theoretical grounding in Piaget and Vygotsky

### Maker Education: Making vs. Tinkering

**27. Martinez, S. L., & Stager, G. (Various). Invent to Learn: Making, Tinkering, and Engineering in the Classroom.**
- **Foundational text** for maker education
- Distinguishes making vs. tinkering approaches

**28. Wilkinson, K., & Petrich, M. The Art of Tinkering.**
- **Making**: Goal-oriented, designing and building specific projects
- **Tinkering**: Playful exploration, rapid prototyping, iteration
  - Emphasizes process over product
  - Celebrates discovery through inquiry
  - "Failing forward" and unconventional material use

**29. Maker Education Core Elements:**
- **Maker Mindset**: Playfulness, experimentation, iteration, collaboration
- **Makerspaces**: Physical/virtual environments with tools (3D printers, Arduino, etc.)
- **Facilitation**: Educator as facilitator rather than content deliverer
- **Assessment**: Process-focused, embedded, performance-based

## Key Themes and Concepts

### 1. Levels of Physical Engagement with Robots

Based on the literature, hands-on activities can be categorized by **level of physical engagement**:

#### A. **Creating/Building** (Highest engagement)
- Designing from scratch
- Creating components
- Making structural decisions
- Highest sense of ownership
- Most creative process

#### B. **Assembling** (High engagement)
- Following instructions/blueprints
- Putting together pre-made components
- Understanding how parts fit together
- IKEA effect applies
- Significant sense of accomplishment

#### C. **Tinkering/Modifying** (Medium-high engagement)
- Taking apart and reassembling
- Modifying existing structures
- Rapid prototyping
- Exploratory, playful approach

#### D. **Programming** (Medium engagement)
- Writing code for existing robot
- Algorithmic thinking
- Less physical, more cognitive
- Can be done on-screen or with physical robot

#### E. **Interacting** (Lower engagement)
- Using pre-assembled, pre-programmed robot
- Social interaction focus
- May increase anthropomorphism
- Less understanding of technical workings

### 2. Psychological Mechanisms

**Ownership Effects (IKEA Effect):**
- Effort invested → higher perceived value
- Sense of ownership → attachment
- Sense of accomplishment → motivation
- Time invested → emotional investment

**Demystification Effects:**
- Building exposes technical features
- Reduces "black box" thinking
- Better understanding of capabilities and limitations
- May reduce anthropomorphism but increase realistic perception

**Learning Through Making:**
- Constructionist approach: knowledge construction through creation
- Problem-solving with direct feedback
- Self-efficacy through overcoming challenges
- Creative process rather than obligation

### 3. Learning Outcomes

**Enhanced by Building/Assembly:**
- Technical literacy
- Understanding of robot capabilities
- Problem-solving skills
- Self-efficacy
- Long-term engagement
- Realistic perception of AI/robots

**Enhanced by Interaction:**
- Social skills
- Communication
- Anthropomorphism (may be benefit or drawback)
- Immediate engagement without technical barriers

### 4. Important Distinctions for BuildBots Study

**Building vs. Assembling:**
- Building = creating from components (higher creativity, decisions)
- Assembling = following instructions (lower creativity, more structured)
- BuildBots uses **assembly** model (following instructions for Marty robot)

**Physical Manipulation vs. Programming:**
- Physical = kinesthetic, tangible, embodied learning
- Programming = algorithmic thinking, can be abstract
- BuildBots combines both (weeks 1-3: assembly, weeks 4-6: programming)

**Hands-On vs. Interaction:**
- Hands-on = active physical engagement with robot structure
- Interaction = social/conversational engagement with functioning robot
- BuildBots contrasts: assembly (experimental) vs. interaction (control)

## Research Gaps and Opportunities

### Gaps Identified:

1. **Limited Research on Children (vs. Adults)**
   - Most IKEA effect studies with adults
   - Few studies on children's attachment through building
   - Williams et al. (2019) is rare example with young children

2. **Building vs. Assembly Not Distinguished**
   - Most papers use terms interchangeably
   - Technical robotics literature distinguishes, but education literature doesn't
   - Need clearer definitions in educational contexts

3. **Long-Term Effects Unknown**
   - Most studies are short-term
   - Need research on lasting effects on:
     - Technical literacy
     - Attachment
     - Continued engagement with technology

4. **Interaction Between Knowledge and Perception**
   - Williams et al. (2019): prior knowledge affects perception
   - Need more research on how building affects this relationship
   - Does building moderate the effect of knowledge on anthropomorphism?

5. **Categorization of Hands-On Activities**
   - Zhong et al. (2022) provides taxonomy, but focused on teaching models
   - Need taxonomy focused on **learner's physical engagement level**
   - Need empirical studies comparing different levels

### BuildBots Study Contributions:

1. **Children-focused** (vs. most adult studies)
2. **Clear distinction**: Assembly (following instructions) vs. Interaction (using pre-built robot)
3. **Longitudinal** design with retention tests
4. **Multiple outcomes**: learning, perception, attachment
5. **Explores moderating effects** of prior knowledge
6. **Real classroom context** (ecological validity)

## Notes for Paper 4 Writing

### Key Framing Points:

1. **Constructionist foundation** (Papert, Piaget, Ackermann)
2. **Hands-on learning effectiveness** (Freeman et al. meta-analysis)
3. **Specificity to robotics/AI** (Hitron, Williams)

5. **Need for categorization** (Zhong taxonomy)
6. **Gap: children's building vs. assembly vs. interaction**

### Contribution Statement:

"While prior work has shown benefits of hands-on robotics activities, few studies have systematically compared different levels of physical engagement (building, assembly, interaction) with robots, particularly with children. Existing research on ownership effects (IKEA effect) has primarily focused on adults, and the distinction between building (creative design) and assembly (following instructions) has not been clearly examined in educational contexts. Furthermore, the interaction between hands-on engagement and children's developing understanding of AI/robot capabilities remains underexplored. The BuildBots study addresses these gaps by comparing robot assembly (following instructions) with robot interaction (using pre-built robots) in a longitudinal, classroom-based study with children, examining effects on learning outcomes, robot perception, and attachment."
